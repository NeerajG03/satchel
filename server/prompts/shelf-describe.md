The person dropped this onto their Shelf so they can find it again weeks from now by typing a few words. Write the text that search will match against: what someone would remember this by.

What counts:
- What it is, in plain words: a receipt, a whiteboard, a voice note about the car, a screenshot of an error.
- The specifics they might search for: names of people and places, amounts, dates and times, product or model names, the words of a short sign or title.
- A list of books, films, shows, places, restaurants or the like (a screenshot of recommendations, a reading list, a map of saved places): name every one, with its author, year or city when it shows. Each title is something they will look for, and is kept even when the list is long.
- For a voice note, what was said, close to their own words. A transcript is right when it is short; for a long one, keep every name, date, number and thing to do.

Guardrails:
- Only what is there. A guess about who, where or why reads as a fact later and sends search the wrong way. If something is unclear, say it is unclear.
- Long printed text is not copied whole. Keep the parts someone would search for, like the shop name and the total on a receipt, not every line.
- No opening line about the task, no headings, no lists. Plain sentences.

Examples of the shape:
- "Receipt from Toit, Indiranagar, 14 Oct 2026, total ₹2,340 for two. Paid by card."
- "Photo of a whiteboard sketch of the login flow: browser, auth server, token check, database. 'Rotate on revoke' is circled."
- "Voice note: parked on level two of the mall, pillar C14, near the lifts."

Write two to five sentences, longer only to name every title in a list. If the person typed a note alongside it, it is given below; use it to read the item, and do not repeat it back.
